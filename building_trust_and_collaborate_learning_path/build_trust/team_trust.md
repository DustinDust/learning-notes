# 2. Prepare for inclusive hybrid team meeting

Author admited her own team messed up first hybrid meeting badly (some ppl remote, some in office) and it hurt team trust. Whole video is checklist to avoid that mistake:

- put BOTH physical room + virtual link in the invite
- send agenda ahead, include inclusive-meeting reminder like "three then me" rule (wait for 3 other ppl to talk before you talk again)
- assign a "gatekeeper" role - someone whose job is noticing imbalance and pulling in quiet voice (eg "hey can we hear from Tito")
- keep camera on so nonverbal cue still visible
- test tech setup BEFORE with someone in room + someone online
- remote folks should see everything onsite folks see: face, deck, whiteboard, handout
- project remote attendee's face BIG on screen in the room (cited research: Bob Frisch & Cary Greene - big image help onsite people remember to include remote folks)
- remote ppl need to PARTICIPATE not just watch: use phone survey tool, let them add to whiteboard before final
- if food provided onsite, offer deliver food to remote folks too
- cute idea: remote person pick an "in-room avatar" - someone onsite they can IM to ask quick question, replicate the "lean over and whisper" thing you lose remotely
- avoid comment that highlight what's MISSING (eg "wish we saw you more around office") instead focus on positive interaction that did happen (eg "thanks for hopping on that call")

# 3. Structure hybrid meetings to build trust

More strategy for building trust specifically in VIRTUAL/hybrid meeting structure (complement previous video which was more about physical/tech setup):

1. socialize : come early, chit chat, maybe 5 min lightning round check-in (weekend, highlight of day)
2. be a "connector" meaning actively introduce people base on shared interest or useful network (author give example script: "hey can you tell her about that article" or "who was that speaker you mentioned, might be good contact for her")
3. leave mic unmuted (unless noisy bg) so conversation flow more natural
4. clarify roles/expectation constantly since virtual team dont have the passive "overhear what other working on" advantage office team has
5. rotate power: let whoever has most knowledge for that stage lead, dont keep power static
6. share timezone burden: dont make same person always take 3am call
7. reliability = single strongest predictor of trust in virtual setting (repeat theme from Course 1), cause "your word is all you have" remotely
