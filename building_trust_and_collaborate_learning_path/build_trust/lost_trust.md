# 1. How to manage a betrayal

Opening examples: colleague steal your idea and pitch as his own, or boss promise promotion then dont deliver AND keep lying about it. Point: everyone get hurt/lied to/let down at some point, question is how to respond.

Key distinction: CARELESS violation vs DELIBERATE betrayal. Someone forgetting to share info ≠ someone actively hiding it from you, both hurt trust but intent matter a lot. Author say notice which one you're ASSUMING happened, and stay open to being wrong about that assumption.

How to actually gauge intent: give person chance to explain, and acknowledge you only see YOUR side. Example: author upset her assistant's hour got cut, she told boss "story i'm telling myself is you dont think my work matter" (callback to earlier "assume positive intent" video's technique). Boss explain it was budget-wide cut, not a personal judgement - didnt fix problem but "took the sting out" once she understood the why.

Deliberate betrayal is harder. Story: Carlton kept looking foolish in meeting cause Sherry hoard info he need, when he confront her she deny it ("i did tell you, you forgot"), he doubt his own memory and let it go... but it happen again and again. Lesson: DATE AND DOCUMENT promise/action, this help you tell one-off carelessness apart from repeated deliberate pattern, and build confidence your reading situation right (not just paranoid).

Response should differ based on which type: for real deliberate betrayer, decrease contact, limit how interdependent your work is with them.

For rebuilding trust after betrayal (from either side), use "dimmer switch" approach (credit: Nan Russell, Psychology Today) - trust isnt binary on/off, rebuild it gradually:

- start: ask for MORE check-in than before ("run this by me before you act")
- after few reliable delivery: ease up a bit ("just keep me posted")
- eventually, if consistent: back to full trust ("just run with it")

# 2. Apologize and make it right

Everyone mess up sometime even with good intentions: forget bday, blurt a secret, break promise, tell white lie. Author reference Stephen Covey's "emotional bank account" metaphor: relationship = account, positive interaction = deposit, negative = withdrawal. As long as you not "overdrawn" (enough deposit built up), 1 withdrawal wont bankrupt relationship. So: keep making deposit (help someone, credit them publicly, confide in them) so account stay well above zero.

When you DO make a withdrawal (violate trust), 5 step from Pat Galagan (Assoc. for Talent Development):

1. admit the breach
2. apologize
3. say what you'll do to prevent it happening again
4. explain how you'll repair the damage
5. take action (actually DO it)

Last emphasis: step 5 (take ACTION) is the one that matter most, "cant talk your way out of situation you acted your way into" - words alone dont rebuild trust, follow-through does.
