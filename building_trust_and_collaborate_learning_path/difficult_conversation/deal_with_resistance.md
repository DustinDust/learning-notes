### 1. Deal with resistance during a difficult conversation

Every difficult conversation has some form of resistance. It means conversation either isnt happening at all, or isnt moving in desired direction.

a. Stuck

Indecision and avoidance before conversation. We dont know how to bring it up, fear their response, and keep procrastinating.To move forward, pick the battle and clarify purpose. Reason why should benefit both people.

b. Attached

Being too attached to how the other person SHOULD respond creates a tug of war. They might reject idea or get emotional. Best to accept and let em go in that case.

c. Negative

Complaining and negativity are signs of resistance. When other person becomes negative, breathe and acknowledge their reality: "I hear you, I know you're frustrated." Then redirect with "What choices do you have?"
Same applies when I catch myself complaining. Finding my choices helps me find some power in situation.

d. Distracted

Avoidance happen before conversation, distraction happen INSIDE it. Other person may blame, get angry or change subject to pull attention away from issue. Once noticed, breathe and say: "Lets refocus on the issue at hand."

=> Exercise: identify situations where I have been stuck, attached, negative or distracted. Awareness makes each form easier to spot next time.

**Personal Notes**

- attachment is interesting as one might accept that conversation is needed but still expect other person to react exactly the way one rehearsed it
- "what choices do I have" is good thinking as complaining usually gets us nowhere and it foils the mood.

### 2. Use radical listening to stay present

Listening is easy when we agree, but becomes more important as conversation gets difficult. Rule: person who listens first controls direction of conversation. `RADICAL LISTENING` means controlling urge to be right, understood and to win.

Main concept is "listen first". Until someone feels heard, they usually keep defending, disagreeing or withdrawing.

How to practice:

1. When something triggers me, take deep breath and create small gap between stimulus and response
2. Acknowledge their reality with "I see", "I hear you" or "I think I understand"
3. Reflect what I understood, such as "I see, so you thought it was a good idea"
4. When they confirm it, pause instead of immediately making my argument
5. Invite more with "say more about that" or "do you still feel that way?"

Watch for shift once they feel understood. Their tone and body language may soften and they may become more curious about my view.

If I need time to process, can end conversation by saying I have more to share and want to continue later.

**Personal Notes**

- big connection to Effective Listening course, specially paraphrasing and allowing silence
- "when is it my turn?" is honestly first thought that comes up for me sometimes. This is enlightening
- radical sounds dramatic lol, but technique itself is just breathing, acknowledging and asking them to continue
- useful reminder that understanding someone's view and agreeing with it are different things

### 3. Avoid resisting resistance

Other person's resistance isnt always biggest problem. Trap happens when their emotional reaction triggers my own and I start resisting them back.

4 A's describe this trap:

a. Anger: they call me unfair, I get angry because they are angry
b. Argument: they say I dont understand, I reply that THEY dont understand
c. Acquiescence: they become uncomfortable, so I immediately give in or change my request
d. Avoidance: I escape conversation before or during it because I dont want to deal with their reaction

3 steps to prevent this:

1. Imagine: before conversation, imagine possible resistance.
1. Create space: if they resist, let them finish.
1. Acknowledge: recognize their perception and ask for clarification.

**Personal Notes**

- acquiescence was new term for me here, basically giving in just to end discomfort (new vocab++)
- preparing for emotional response doesnt mean assuming worst intention. It just reduces surprise
- not soothing someone feels counterintuitive, but I think author mean give emotion space instead of rushing to remove it. Basically allow them to feel what they feel.
- 4 A's are mostly different ways of losing control of my own response

### 4. Learn the magic phrase to test for resistance

Resistance is negativity or non acceptance, basically unwillingness to move forward. It sounds like:

- "This isnt fair"
- "I dont have enough time"
- "That isnt possible"
- "It would cost too much"
- "I already tried that"

Willingness is the turning point for change. Because change brings discomfort, some resistance should be expected. Magic phrase to test it: "Would you be willing?"

Technique is to avoid debating blame or getting distracted. Acknowledge each concern, stay with original request and keep testing willingness.

**Personal Notes**

- phrase gives person a choice while also making them answer actual request
- could become annoying if repeated like robot, tone and real listening probably matter a lot here
- combines the redirect technique with radical listening: acknowledge reality, then return to purpose

### 5. Working with people who are not willing to change

Most difficult conversations are about asking someone to change or repairing our own past mistake. Conversation is only starting point though. Cant force change and cant force forgiveness.

If person remains unwilling, think ABC:

Acceptance: accepting situation may be best choice when I have little power. Still have conversation first because person cant choose to change if they dont even know there is problem.

Boundaries: boundary describes what I will do, not how I will force them to act.

Change: if acceptance and boundary arent enough, make bigger change such as moving department or leaving company.

=> Even when someone doesnt cooperate, I still have 3 choices: accept, set boundary, or make a change.

**Personal Notes**

- boundary being about MY next action is a nice focus. Otherwise it becomes another demand that depends on person cooperating
- acceptance sounds passive but can be intentional temporary choice, specially when there isnt good option right now
- conversation still matters even if I expect a no
