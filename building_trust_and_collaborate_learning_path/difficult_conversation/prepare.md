### 1. Prepare for a difficult conversation

2 steps to prepare:

a. Get Clear about the situation

Ask 2 questions:

1. What is this person doing that they should not be doing?
2. What are they not doing that they should be doing?

b. Clear up negative emotions

Notice the negative thoughts and labels used for the other person, such as lazy, ignorant, stubborn or passive aggressive. Feeling annoyed isnt wrong, but bringing all that resentment into conversation creates a barrier.

Move attention away from their perceived flaw and think about how conversation could benefit them. Some calming thoughts:

- they are human and make mistakes like everyone
- they deserve a chance to improve
- this situation may help me grow as well

=> Dont initiate until I can describe situation clearly and let go of the labels as much as possible.

**Personal Notes**

- good test for vague feedback: can I point to exact behavior, or do I only have an adjective like "lazy"?
- the 2 questions give both current problem and desired next action. Just telling someone to stop isnt enough
- neutralizing emotion doesnt mean pretending nothing bothered me. More like making sure irritation isnt driving the whole conversation. In other words: detachment for a bit

### 2. Control the direction of the difficult conversation

We sometimes avoid difficult conversation because we're afraid of losing control. Other person says something distracting, conversation takes a turn and suddenly its verbal ping pong about everything except original problem.

Before inviting them, clarify:

a. Primary purpose: the main behavior or result that needs to change
b. Secondary purpose: what else I need to understand

Possible redirects:

- "I understand you were joking, but I'm asking you not to joke about my work." -> When others are being sarcastic about your work
- "Maybe I am being sensitive. I'm still asking you to respect my wish and stop." -> When others are being passive aggressive
- "Maybe other people are okay with it. I'm asking you to stop doing it to me." -> Set clear bounndaries

Preparation exercise:

1. Identify primary purpose
2. Identify secondary purpose
3. List possible distractions
4. Prepare ways to redirect each one

### 3. Avoid exaggeration and escalation in difficult conversations

4 ways to avoid:

a. Take responsibility for my emotional experience

E.g: "I am frustrated" means I own my feeling. "Lisa makes me frustrated" places all responsibility on Lisa.

b. Separate the emotion from the issue
c. Ask for one behavior I want
d. Use specific and observable behavior

=> Specific behavior keeps conversation about what happened and what should change, instead of counting who's technically right.

**Personal Notes**

- I use exaggeration casually all the time, but in conflict people can grab onto one inaccurate word and ignore main point. This is the habit I should avoid in professional settings
- owning my emotion feels very selfhelp-y, but it's good thing to remember
