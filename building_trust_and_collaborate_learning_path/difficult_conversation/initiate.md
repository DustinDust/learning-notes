### 1. Learn the blueprint for a difficult conversation

There is no single way to handle every difficult conversation, but author give a 5 step blueprint:

1. State the purpose
2. Present observations
3. Listen and invite feedback
4. State the request
5. Create accountability

a. State the purpose

Explain what the conversation is about and why you're bringing it up.

b. Present observations

Share facts and specific behavior, without guessing their motive. Tone should be neutral, not judgemental or threatening.

c. Listen and invite feedback

At some point the other person need to speak. Some ways to invite them:

- "What are your thoughts?"
- "Can you fill me in on whats going on?"
- "Walk me through your process."

Then actually LISTEN. If they start giving excuses or blaming, acknowledge emotion without losing the purpose.

d. State the request

Say clearly what you want them to do or stop doing. Can say it directly or ask for agreement:

- "I need you to work with Rob for 2 days next week."
- "Would you be willing to work with Rob for 2 days so he can train you?"

e. Create accountability

Agreement isnt the end. Set a clear follow up, such as meeting again in 2 weeks to review documentation and progress.

=> Blueprint doesnt guarantee cooperation, but it helps keep conversation focused on intended result.

**Personal Notes**

- this combines almost everything from previous section: clear purpose, observable behavior, listening, direct request and follow up
- accountability part is easy to forget when conversation feels like it went well.
- sounds a little scripted on paper but having structure is probably useful when nerves kick in

### 2. Find your why in a difficult conversation

Before difficult conversation we ask "is this worth it?" and get stuck between what we want and what we dont want:

- want employee to improve, dont want to hurt their feeling
- want to be honest with spouse, dont want them angry
- want colleague to stop inappropriate jokes, dont want to lose friendship

Author call this INNER CONFLICT. Need to resolve it before talking with the other person.

2 steps:

a. Focus on what I want
b. Find a compelling reason why

Template:

1. I want to have a conversation about...
2. My big picture reason why is...

**Personal Notes**

- useful distinction between little picture and big picture. Short discomfort can protect the relationship or help someone improve in long term
- "I dont want them to be upset" sound caring but can also be an excuse to avoid conversation
- this why should be honest though. Saying "I care about your success" just to soften criticism would feel fake pretty quickly
- connects back to team's mission in foundation section, clear reason gives somewhere to return when conversation goes off track
