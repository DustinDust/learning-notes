### 1. Six key questions for effective collaboration

**Summary**

Main idea: team need strong foundation before jumping into actual work, same like building a house need foundation first. Framework is 6 basic question: who, what, why, where, when, how. Author says most team just skip straight to "doing" without clarifying these (not my team), and that's mistake. Taking 1-2 meeting at start to go thru these question force team to set boundary, define expectation, and catch disagreement early instead of later.

!! Big warning from author: this is NOT a check-the-box exercise, the value is in the discussion/journey itself, not in having nice deliverable at the end. So team should actually dig into disagreement, not just rush thru it.

Some example of "digging in":

- if some ppl love video call and other never turn camera on - dig into why, might be style/preference difference
- if some prioritize customer interaction and other avoid it - might mean team not aligned on goal/priority
- if some expect WFH and other want in person meeting - clarify early

### 2. Identify the right team composition

This is deep dive into the "who" question. Question to ask about team:

- skillset and experience?
- authority level, is there decision maker on team (and do we even need one)?
- availability?
- enough diversity? (?????). I think here the author just means have enough roles and styles for the project to not feel "boring"?
- complementary work style, worked together before, do they even get along

Acknowledge reality tho: often you CANT pick your teammate. But you can still assess gap and try close it:

- negotiate part-time support (eg intern w/ specific skill)
- pursue training for skill gap
- outsource some task if resource constrained

### 3. Clarify the team's goal and purpose

This one about "what" (goal/task) and "why" (mission, or "so what" - the thing that make people actually CARE).

Author share own bad experience: media client project, newer team member leading a milestone session had very different vision than rest of team, they discussed but never actually reached agreement. Result: disaster in front of client, couldnt answer client question cause team wasnt in sync, damaged credibility.

=> Takeaway: team need clarity on what+why to succeed.

Technique suggested: gives everyone a card, one side write their understanding of team GOAL, other side write team MISSION. Collect & read & identify issues/mistunderstanding/confusion about each category, which we can work together to clarify. Most team skip this and pay price later.

### 4. Clarify constraints, expectations, and processes up front

Covers last 3 question: where, when, how.

**Where**: consider remote employee, satellite location, meeting location logistic. Author give example: client whose meeting always at HQ where parking bad, so half team late 10-15 min EVERY meeting, small thing that becomes recurring big thing bc never discussed upfront.

**When**: working hours, timezone issue, meeting cadence, key deliverable timeline. Example: weekly newsletter team need to discuss handoff timing specifically, before stuff start falling thru crack.

**How**: author call this "the silent killer", which is easiest thing to skip cause less "obvious" than goal/task planning, but wont happen automatically (no osmosis) and definitely wont happen efficiently if skipped. When team dont clarify how, everyone default to own preferred method: some come to office voluntarily, other call in from home; some send detailed status update, other just text if problem happen.

Tip if stuck: think back to past collaboration problem you personally experienced, and have team proactively address those specific issue.
