### 1. Develop team ground rules

4 step process for making ground rules that actually stick, work whether your leader or just regular member.

1. Explain WHY ground rule matter first, dont just jump into it. Author give sample script: basically "we've all been on dysfunctional team before, dont want repeat that, success hinge on collaborating well so worth spending a bit of time on this"
2. Give everyone post-it pad + marker, ask them think about best/worst team experience and write ONE ground rule they think would help
3. After few min, collect post-its, review each one with whole team out loud. Example of what people write: "start/end meeting on time", "avoid email back-and-forth volleyball", "no conference call after 2pm friday", even fun one like "any meeting over 2hr must have food" (author say team LOVED that one)
4. Vote on each rule, only adopt ones everybody actually support
   Key point: process must be BOTTOM UP. If leader (or even 1 team member) try to force rules onto team, it wont work, doesnt stick. Rules that team co-create themselves are the one that actually get followed.

### 2. Build relationships early and often

Open with relatable scenario: hot day, AC broken, someone cuts you off in traffic, your instantly mad... until you realize its your friend, then mood totally shift. Point: situation same, but perspective change cause of relationship. Same true at work, relationship = huge factor in how well people collaborate.

Strong relationship benefit: not just "more enjoyable to work together" (tho that help), but also:

- more likely to go extra mile helping someone
- more willing to admit "i dont understand this"
- give benefit of doubt when communication is ambiguous/odd
- conflict resolve easier

=> How to build it: INCREMENTAL approach, infuse relationship-building slowly + consistently into everyday team stuff, not just big one-off event.

Retreat/happy hour/volunteer day = great but not enough alone ("cant microwave relationship building"). Need small everyday touch too. 2 example:

1. During meeting intro, ask ppl share first paid job or random little-known fact - adds levity, find commonality, break down hierarchy barrier (eg finding out your VP used to bag groceries same as you = instant connection)
2. "Bagel Thursday" from author's old tech company job: every thurs 2pm, whole office stop and go grab bagel/cream cheese in one room, chat random 20 min. Author say she'd often bump into ppl from other dept, learn about new project, or catch up w someone she hadnt seen. Simple but effective for reinforcing connection regularly.

### 3. Identify team members' preferred communication mode

First off: Eeryone hardwired toward a preferred communication mode (face to face, phone, email or text), and while we naturally default to OUR OWN preferred mode when reaching others, people respond faster/fuller if you use THEIR preferred mode instead.

=> Need to figure out how to find out someone's preference: just ask.

Example pet peeves given:

- email group: hate when request sent to multiple ppl and nobody clear who's actually owning it
- text group: hate people randomly popping up at their desk with quick question, would rather get text instead
  Benefit isnt just faster task completion, also help team "gel" better overall.

**Personal Notes**

- "just ask" being considered as rare/underused: True lol sometimes pepole kind of just ignore the fact that they can just go up and ask "hey what's your go-to method to communication? I like my message to be read asap in the future."
- pet peeve sharing part seems very valuable, but is hard in some culture (for example my own)
- this pairs well with ground-rule video: could literally turn "everyone's preferred comm mode" into an actual team ground rule after doing this exercise

### 4. Tips for great online collaboration

Most team today cant collaborate well without tools, specially as more team member work virtual. Tools needed for scheduling, task/project management, and daily collab in general. Bad news is... theres so many tool to pick from.

Few category of tool worth having:

1. Content/knowledge management system: central online repo for sharing info, managing doc, version control.
2. Calendaring system
3. Polling/survey tool
4. Video conferencing

   !Remeber: dont think tool alone will "do the collaboration work for you"

### 5. Take periodic temperature checks

we often stay heads-down doing work without pausing to check how things actually going / what need change. Other industry do the same thing as well:

- healthcare: M&M (morbidity and mortality) session
- military: after-action review
- pro sports: reviewing game tape before next practice

  Best workplace team do similar: PERIODIC "temperature check" to see whats working / what to change.

Why it matters: things constantly changing (people cycle in/out, process changes, new project), so what worked before might not work now. Cadence can be quarterly/monthly/whatever fits, just need SOME regular check-in mechanism, and actually act on it if somethings off.
