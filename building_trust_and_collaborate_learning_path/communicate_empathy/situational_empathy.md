### 1. Learn the key empathy behaviors

Empathy can be difficult when people differ in class, education, politics, ethnicity, generation etc. Course gives 3 behaviors for making it practical:

1. Patience
2. Perspective
3. Connection

House analogy:

- patience is foundation that keeps us emotionally steady and present
- perspective is walls that help us see and hear clearly without judgement
- connection is doors that bring different rooms and people together

Patience helps with stress and keeps attention on person in front of us. Perspective reminds us we never have full context and should pause before speaking. We dont only hear what people say, we hear our OPINION of what they say.

Connection means treating every conversation as new opportunity for relationship to grow. Approach it like it could be first and last talk with that person.

**Personal Notes**

- simple framework, patience controls reaction, perspective challenges assumption, connection moves relationship forward
- house analogy is a bit much but makes order clear. Connection is difficult without first two
- the line about hearing our opinion of words is very true during conflict

### 2. Empathy tip: Practicing patience

Empathy matters most during stressful moment, like bad review, possible layoff or disrespectful comment.

Practice has 2 steps:

1. Take a deep breath
2. Repeat what person said back to them

Breath isnt only for releasing tension. It forces a pause so first emotional response doesnt come straight out and escalate situation.

Repeating their words checks whether I heard correctly, missed something or misunderstood intention. It also reduces listening bias because I have to say their actual point instead of my interpretation.

=> Patience is pausing to avoid escalation and seeking clarity to avoid confusion.

### 3. Empathy tip: Pursuing perspective

Coworker could arrive late or communicate badly because of childcare issue, caring for sick parent, witnessing accident, or many things I know nothing about.

Pursuing perspective means remembering I never have complete context for someone's behavior.

First identify gaps in own experience. Background, education, family, hobbies and life choices create specific worldview. My perspective is valuable because others dont know what I know, and opposite is true too.

Knowing what I know helps reveal what I DONT know. Then I can stay open and learn instead of treating my experience as default for everyone.

=> Pursuing perspective is curiosity in action.

### 4. Empathy tip: Collaborative connection

Collaborative connection needs both closed and open questions.

a. Closed questions establish facts and clarity

Usually who, what, where and when:

- What time is meeting?
- Who is on project?
- When is next phase due?
- Where can we improve?

b. Open questions create deeper conversation

Usually why and how:

- Why do you feel this way?
- How did your experience shape your opinion?
- Why did my comment offend you?
- How can I help?

Closed questions create solid ground. Open questions build bridge by allowing thought, experience and vulnerability to be shared.

=> Get clarity first, then use it as foundation for deeper connection.

### 5. Empathy tip: Communicating with empathy in groups

Its harder to empathize with coworkers we cant relate to. Differences become exaggerated, quiet person becomes "weak" and less friendly person becomes "awful".

Groups can fall into tribal thinking where unfamiliar people feel like threat. Modern threat may only be looking bad in meeting or losing credit, but reaction is still attack or escape.

How to break group standstill:

1. Acknowledge my tribal habits and check gut reaction toward other group
2. Reduce number of people involved
3. Let one representative from each side discuss issue with patience, perspective and connection
4. Once they agree, each person explains other side to their own team

Two people can usually focus on solution better than 10 people defending their group. Teammates may also accept opposing view more easily from representative they trust.

### 6. Communicate with empathy when you hold different beliefs

We dont choose coworkers based on shared values like we often do with friends, so workplace is where empathy gets properly tested.

4 competencies:

1. Emotions: Keep them in check because feeling affects thought, words and body language. Remember person I disagree with still has family, stress, fear and many emotions similar to mine.

1. Why: Know reason for conversation. The why acts like guiding light when emotion pulls discussion off course.

1. Tone: Speak in way other person can receive, not only way I personally prefer to be spoken to. Tone includes voice and overall direction of conversation.

1. Listening: Really listen to experience even when view feels offensive or frustrating. Listening creates perspective, which then makes connection possible.

=> Disagreement doesnt remove need to work together. Lean into discomfort enough to understand, without needing to adopt same belief.

### 7. Audit your empathetic communication

Empathy is practice, so it needs regular review like any other skill.

4 step audit:

1. Assess current state: Review communication style, empathy behaviors and situational spectrum. Think about recent conversations, own feelings, people's reactions and feedback received.

1. Decide where I want to be: Return to earlier goals and update them as I improve. Target should evolve instead of staying fixed forever.

1. Identify gaps: Compare current behavior with desired one and find specific area where I'm falling short.

1. Plan next steps: Choose empathy behavior or habit that directly addresses current gap.

Dont try fixing everything at once. Pick only 1 or 2 habits, practice them and review again later.

**Personal Notes**

- this turns vague goal "be more empathetic" into observable improvement plan
- useful inputs are self reflection, actual reactions and feedback from others, not only one source
- focusing on 1 or 2 habits seems much more realistic than trying to change whole communication style at once
