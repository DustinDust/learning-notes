### 1. Communicate with empathy online

Much of modern communication happens through email, Slack, text, social media and video calls rather than in person. Before sending something online, ask:

1. Would I actually say this to person face to face?
2. What is this adding to conversation?

Digital distance makes it easier to type hurtful thing because other person doesnt feel fully real. But there is still actual human receiving it. Written words also carry tone even when we dont intend one.

Sometimes text is wrong channel and video call is needed. Video still isnt same as being together physically though:

- network lag may create awkward interruptions
- image may be unclear
- person may have distraction around them
- eye contact can look strange because camera and screen are in different place
- much of body language isnt visible

Dont assume these technical limitations mean disrespect or lack of interest. Patience is especially important when technology fails or I feel ignored.

### 2. Communicate with empathy across generations

Age gap can affect communication preference and worldview. Prepare same way as any other conversation: know own style, recognize when empathy becomes difficult and plan for those moments.

3 important practices:

a. Value multiple generational perspectives

Older coworkers have more years of experience and can recognize patterns. Younger coworkers experience current world from perspective older people cant recreate firsthand. Both views are valuable, specially when building products for different people.

b. Use different communication channels

Words and tone arent enough, format also matter. Some people dislike texting and private messages, while others avoid email or phone call.

Speak to people in way they can best receive message. Need flexibility in delivery, not only content.

c. Respect their lived experience without dismissing mine

Younger doesnt automatically mean more capable because of current trend knowledge. Older doesnt automatically mean wiser because of industry experience. Empathy comes from pursuing both perspectives without turning age into competition.

=> People are more willing to share and learn from me when I also show real interest in learning from them.

### 3. Communicate with empathy in a globally connected world

Technology gives us access to people across cultures and continents, but creates more chances for message to get lost in translation. Jokes, cultural reference, nuanced language and idioms may not travel well. Differences can feel intimidating, but empathy principles stay same.

3 practices:

a. Understand my own culture

Culture shapes worldview and attitude, even though people from same home can still have completely different experience. Awareness of my background helps identify assumptions I carry and makes it easier to learn another perspective.

b. Be genuinely curious

Ask and answer questions, including ones that seem basic. My lived experience isnt universal, and other person may be equally unfamiliar with my culture.

Connect because of difference and what can be learned from it, not only despite it. Empathy dies in willful ignorance.

c. Consider individual listener

Culture matters but doesnt completely define person. They still have individual personality, style and communication preference.

Put humanity at center by adapting message to actual listener rather than communicating with stereotype of their culture.

**Personal Notes**

- understanding my own culture is easy to skip because own habits usually feel "normal" instead of cultural
- avoiding idioms is practical, specially in international teams where English may be second or third language
- curiosity need to be respectful and relevant, not treating colleague like representative for entire country
- culture gives context, individual person still gives final answer on how to communicate with them
