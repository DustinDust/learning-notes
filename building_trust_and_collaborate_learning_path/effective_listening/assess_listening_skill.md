## 1. Five skills for improved listening

**Summary**

Intro video, ask you to self rank 5 listening skill/intention from 1 (best) to 5 (weakest) before course dive into each one:

1. recalling detail: remembering name, date, specific info (good at Trivial Pursuit type thing)
2. understanding big picture: grasp overall meaning even without recalling detail
3. evaluating content: critical listening, not just accepting what speaker say
4. attending to subtle cue: nonverbal stuff, facial expression, body language, tone of voice
5. empathize: understand emotional state of speaker, being "emotional detective" as she call it

   Rest of course go thru each of these one by one, why it matters and tips to improve weakest one.

**Notes**

- self rank exercise is good initiate, makes u think of your weak points
- overlaps a lot with "Listen and empathize" section from Building Trust course haha.

### 2. Recall details

This the "boss give multi step instruction" or "date you need to act on" type of listening. Key question before you even start listening:

- Do I need to act on this info now or later
- What do I need to remember to take that action. If the answer is no actions needed, then the big picture or empathy listening is more appropriate instead.

- Listen to weather forecast without watching, try recall daily temp for the week
- Take notes the next time someone give you task instruction (eg tech support walking you thru something), then test yourself reading those notes back to them or just try following your own notes.

  **Notes**

- the "do i need to act on this" filter is useful. Sometimes I do over-listen
- weather forecast recall exercise is cheap and repeatable

---

### 3. Understand the big picture

Here detail can actually be a distraction, sometimes grasping the broader vision/direction is better.

Test: can you sit thru REALLY long meeting then summarize it into 1-2 sentence afterwards?

Tips to improve this type:

- Ask yourself "how will this matter 5 year from now" and "how would i explain this to outsider who dont know the context".
- Practice by attending lectures then list out key idea after, if you got more than 3-4 main idea you was probably too focus on detail. Best to condense it into like 1 or 2 only.

  **Notes**

- nice pairing with skill #2, basically opposite listening mode, and picking right one for situation is the whole point of this course

---

### 4. Evaluate content

Critical/judging listening - forming opinion, weighing proposal, deciding between option => Not just listen and remember but also have to think about and understand the thing you listened.

Question to ask myself while evaluate listening:

- Is the speaker making viable arguments?
- Is evidence recent, credible, or relevant to the topic?
- Are both pros and cons presented, or is it only one side?
- Any misleading info / fallacy in the argument?
- "if this was a product, would I buy it"

!! Caution: dont get distracted by the delivery of the speaker (charisma, accent etc), stay focus on content only.

**Notes**

- "if this was a product would i buy it" is nice and simple but i think its hard to apply to most of my listening situation.
- Warning about not being swayed by delivery/charisma is important, but delivery matters too.

---

### 5. Attune to subtle cues

About reading nonverbal signal like body language, tone, thing left unsaid. This will help you catch more nuance in the content you're listening to

Tips to practice:

- Watch people converse in language you dont understand, or watch soap opera and try figure out the plot just from nonverbal.
- Walk into meeting where you dont know anyone, try guess who's whose boss based on cue alone.
- If you're not naturally good at this, get a "nonverbal interpreter" like someone who know the context/culture/people, debrief with them after

  **Notes**

- "nonverbal interpreter" is a nice thought but in practice: just talk to people

---

### 6. Listen with empathy

Empathetic listener mirror emotion/body language of speaker, laser focused, listen and paraphrase both emotion AND content (not just content). Useful for conflict situation.

How to do it:

- Mentally put yourself in their shoes, immerse fully without judging or getting distracted
- mirror nonverbal
- Use acknowledging response like "i see", "uh-huh"
- Use sentence stem: "tell me more about that", "i can see why you feel upset", "i'd be frustrated too", "what happened next"
- Avoid discounting phrase like "that's not that bad", "dont get so upset", "it'll be fine"

!Caution: empathetic listening can be emotionally draining if you not used to it, so practice on lower stakes situation first (eg someone else's problem, not yours - listen to soccer mom complain about coach, not your own kid's coach)
