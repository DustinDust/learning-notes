### 2. Defining diplomacy and tact

Author had several options while listening to Karen: could openly show disapproval and ask her to stop, or give nervous smile and dismiss it. Here, `TACT` is about behavior and balance of multiple skills:

- attentive listening
- rapport
- politeness
- empathy, which is the most important one

`DIPLOMACY` is the situational barometer or mindset used to analyze whats happening. It helps decide which skills are needed and what consequences a response may have.

Not every situation need deep tact and diplomacy. Workplace conflict is different, since how you respond to situations will affect your working relationship with your colleagues. Soft skills also have strong impact on workplace reputation. Course mention survey of almost 700 HR professionals:

- 97% would hire applicant with strong soft skills and weaker technical ability
- only 9% favored strong technical skills with weak interpersonal skills
- tact and diplomacy ranked around 60th percentile of importance, after organization and verbal communication

SSUMMARY:
=> Tact is how I behave. Diplomacy is how I read the situation and choose my approach.

### 3. Describing the benefits of diplomacy and tact

Tactless behavior usually looks like:

- Acting quickly without concern for other people
- Communicating too directly with "no filter"
- Speaking before thinking
- Preferring debate over dialogue

Tactful and diplomatic behavior looks like:

- pausing before speaking, specially in difficult situation
- listening carefully and showing empathy
- trying to understand other person's perspective
- diffusing conflict instead of completely avoiding it

Culture decides what diplomacy looks like. Some cultures prefer indirect request and feedback, so very assertive style may feel rude. In more direct culture, diplomatic wording can sound vague or wishy washy. This means diplomacy require flexibility. Need to read each environment, understand people involved and adjust communication while still remaining tactful.
