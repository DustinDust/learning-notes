### 1. The tact diplomacy model introduction

In delicate situation, we are either initiating message or reacting to one.

- When initiating, we ENCODE or formulate the message.
- When reacting, we DECODE or translate what someone sent us. This is harder when message is unexpected or creates emotional response.

Tact and diplomacy model has 4 parts:

1. Formulate or translate the message
2. Add right level of politeness for culture and situation
3. Consider timing
4. Choose actual words, tone, approach and channel

Need to avoid misreading verbal and nonverbal cues. Lack of eye contact, for example, can mean disrespect in one culture and respect in another. When unsure, directly ask for clarification.

**Personal Notes**

- useful first question: am I creating the message or trying to understand one?
- most parts happen quickly in real conversation, but model gives a checklist to practice beforehand
- culture affects almost every step, not only politeness. Be aware

### 2. Formulating messages

Encoding needs clarity, simplicity and empathy for audience. Put myself in their shoes before sending anything sensitive.

For established professional relationship, consider:

- Is this relationship worth nurturing?
- Do we need to build trust?
- Do I want a win win result?

With someone I dont know well, use a soft opener to build rapport. Would only take few seconds and make message easier to receive.

Encoding also include channel and context, not only words. Clicking thumbs up on post about someone's dead pet is tactless even if intention was supportive. Better options could be thoughtful comment, condolences in person or handwritten card depending on relationship.

=> Clear message + empathy + correct channel + context = more diplomatic encoding.

**Personal Notes**

- soft opener isnt useless small talk here, it prepares person for sensitive message and recognizes their position
- reaction button example shows same symbol can communicate something very different depending on context
- the more personal or sensitive message is, the more personal channel should probably be

### 3. Translating messages

Decoding means listening closely to words and meaning, while also noticing sender's nonverbal behavior.

Questions to ask:

1. What might sender intend?
2. How do they understand situation?
3. Is there past history affecting this?
4. Are their verbal and nonverbal messages conflicting?

Sometimes message is direct, like Karen openly calling Pat incompetent. Other times we need clarification. If Karen had only asked what author thought of Pat, author could respond: "I've worked with him for almost 5 years. Why do you ask?"

Messages also have "subtitles": gestures, posture, facial expression, silence and overall tone.

Culture can change their meaning. Silent listener with strong eye contact may show respect in Swedish business setting, while same behavior could look like disapproval in Southern European culture. A cultural interpreter can help when needed.

When message isnt clear, ask directly: "I'm sorry, I'm not sure I understand."

Tóm lại:

=> Take time, consider context and clarify instead of confidently mistranslating someone.

**Personal Notes**

- this connects to subtle cues from Effective Listening, with extra warning that cultural meaning isnt universal
- asking for clarification might feel awkward for 5 seconds, but wrong assumption can last much longer

### 4. Being polite

Politeness includes courtesy, empathy, good manners and positive intent. Small wording changes affect how request feels:

1. "Do you have the time?"
2. "Would you mind telling me the time, please?"
3. "I'm sorry to bother you, do you happen to have the time?"

2 forms of politeness:
a. Positive politeness: Build connection through familiarity, similarity or compliments. "Would you mind telling me the time, please?" is framed as kind request.

b. Negative politeness: Recognize that request may impose on person and show deference.

Culture matters here too. Gift may be accepted immediately in US, while Japanese host may refuse it several times first. Smiling and nodding can mean agreement in one culture but only listening in another.

**Personal Notes**

- confusing name because "negative politeness" isnt rude, it means respecting someone's space and inconvenience
- too much polite wording could sound weird in direct culture, so again have to read situation

### 5. Timing your message effectively

Timing means deciding whether to respond now or later. If later, even time of day, week or quarter can affect result.

Some situation need immediate tact. At busy car rental counter, author politely asked employee to skip package explanations and use existing reservation because she had long drive ahead. Waiting to complain later wouldnt solve current problem.

=> Timing depends on purpose, people present and whether immediate action is needed.

**Personal Notes**

- immediate vs later isnt about courage. Sometimes waiting is more diplomatic because privacy or more info is needed
- useful check: will speaking now solve something, or only release my emotion?
- timing and channel are closely linked, specially when conflict starts through email

### 6. Crafting your message tactfully

Final message should focus on audience. Consider relationship, power dynamic, stakes and whether interaction is temporary or ongoing.

3 main decisions:
a. Approach - Direct approach fits urgency, safety issue or need for immediate action. Indirect approach can save face and support collaboration.

b. Tone - Diplomatic tone should be soft, sincere, gracious and empathetic. Formality depends on audience. Nonverbals need to match, with relaxed expression and suitable smile. Full toothy smile isnt appropriate for every serious request obviously.

c. Channel - Choose between face to face, email, call, text, voice message or written document. Channel changes tone and how message will be understood. Email and text easily create misunderstanding.

=> Good message accounts for audience, approach, tone and channel. Every situation needs slightly different calculation.

**Personal Notes**

- direct can still be tactful. Tact doesnt automatically mean adding lots of vague language
- indirect approach makes more sense where relationship continues and person need chance to save face
- face and tone matching words connect back to authenticity course. Polite sentence with annoyed expression wont land as polite
- channel choice is part of message itself, not just delivery detail
