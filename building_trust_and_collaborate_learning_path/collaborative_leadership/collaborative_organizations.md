### 1. The need for collaboration

Companies still need talented people, but individual talent alone isnt enough anymore. Competitive advantage also depends on how well people work TOGETHER.

Why collaboration matters:

1. Better customer experience: Customers expect service to feel seamless and personalized. This requires different people and departments to coordinate instead of passing customer around disconnected systems.

1. More innovation: Creativity benefits from collective diversity, which means combining ideas from people with different background, thinking styles and perspectives across organization.

1. Higher employee satisfaction: New generation of workers want more communication, more control and more collaboration in how they work.

Biggest obstacle is SILO MENTALITY. A team, department, product line or region becomes silo when it focuses only inward. People develop "just let me do my job" attitude and ignore how partnership could improve result for entire organization.

Silo mentality creates power struggle and kills knowledge sharing.

Practical action for team meeting:

- review benefits of collaboration
- discuss which other areas team supports or influences
- invite people from those areas into conversation

=> Collaboration moved from nice corporate idea to necessary business strategy.

### 2. Collaborating across the organization

Leaders need to understand how organization encourages collaboration and model those behaviors inside their own teams.

5 organizational strategies:

a. Reward collaboration

Companies can include collaboration in performance reviews, recognition and promotion decisions. Team leader should thank people for their contribution and recognize members who actively help others.

b. Focus on innovation

Cross functional teams bring different perspectives together. Leaders support creativity by treating failure as learning experience instead of automatically punishing it. Sharing lessons from own mistakes makes it safer for others to do same.

c. Communicate transparently

Information can enable collaboration or block it. Team needs to trust leader to be open and candid. If something cant be discussed yet, say that honestly rather than pretending not to know.

d. Collaborate with customers

Organizations learn through survey, customer panel and feedback shared across company. Even without external customer contact, team can ask internal customers how well their needs are being met.

e. Redefine leadership

Collaborative leadership shifts from "command and control" toward "influence and include." Leader helps people build relationship, feel valued and contribute ideas toward common goal.

Questions to reflect on:

1. What am I currently doing to support collaborative culture?
2. What could I do better?
3. Does my own behavior show the collaboration I expect from others?

**Personal Notes**

- reward system matters because people follow what gets recognized, not only what company values page says
- openly sharing failure links collaboration with psychological safety and authenticity
- saying "I cant discuss that right now" is more trustworthy than obvious fake "I dont know"
- internal customer idea is useful for engineering teams, since other teams depend on tools and system we create
- influence and include sounds better than command and control, but leader still need to make clear decisions when required
