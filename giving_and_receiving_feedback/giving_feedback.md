# The five feedback steps

## Step 1: Prepare

- Sometimes people ask for feedback on the spot, thats fine if youre ready, but it's also okay to say "let me think about it" and take some time to gather your thoughts
- When preparing, think about:
  - what key info you want to share
  - How to deliver it
  - Concrete examples

## Step 2: Your tone

- Often it's not about what you say, its how you say it that matters
- Keep tone supportive and informal, suggest way to overcome
- Be approachable and empathetic, help the person find solutions instead of just dwelling on what went wrong

## Step 3 Explore facts and use concrete examples

- Sometimes people just ask u for opinions and that's fine too, but for formal feedback, use real examples
- Facts make feedback feel less personal and more objective
- Tips: use multiple examples, not just 1 single event. Be clear about what's fact vs what's ur personal opinions

## Step 4 Listen

- Feedback should be both ways and collaborative
- The other person might have different perspective that could change your view
- Give them time to digest information, ask for the perspective and listen

## Create a forward focus

- Help the person see the feedback as data point they can use moving forward
- Use facts and examples, but focus on future opportunities
- Help them figure out how to use the info to improve

# Break the bias when providing feedback

## Main idea

When we get feedback that includes both good and potential improvement areas we tend to fixate only on the negativity. This is just how our brain is wired and we gotta break it

## Why our brain does this

- Our part that receive negative stimulus use more energy so once brain spots something bad, it gets stored into long-term memory faster and longer.
- This helps survival back in the day, but isn't really useful now if you want to improve healthily

## What this means

- If you give someone both positive and negative feedback, most people will focus on the negative.
  => You gotta think about the balance. It is researched that "it takes 6 pieces of positive feedback to balance out just 1 piece of negative feedback"

## Tips to beat the negativity bias

- Plan your feedback to include good balance, acknowledge what went well and where there's room to improve
- Avoid the "negative sandwich" (positive, then negative, then positive again), this was traditionally used to hide the negatives but it's better to just be honest
- Be empathetic, think about how the other person might feel.
- Don't assume you have all the answers just because you have some experience or perspective
- Don't avoid difficult feedback just because it's uncomfortable. Sometimes the only feedback you have IS about improvement, that's fine, just be direct, clear and empathetic about it
- If you genuinely don't have anything useful to add, it's okay to just say that, don't force feedback just for the sake of it, authenticity matters more
